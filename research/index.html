Research · measurement-first security · PhD applicant

Does critical infrastructure actually move to post-quantum cryptography, or does it only look like it has?

I measure the post-quantum migration rather than design schemes for it. The interesting failures are operational: classical-only fallback, silent downgrades, and rollouts that terminate at the edge where the constrained devices live. Crypto-agility, not scheme design.

I am applying to fully funded PhD programmes to work on this full time, with an advisor who pushes on the parts I get wrong. First-author IEEE publication (DOI), M.S. in Cyber Forensics from Sacred Heart University.

Current work

Measurement in progress. Stated plainly, including what is not finished.

Cross-protocol PQC readiness

Built a scanner that measures post-quantum readiness across TLS, SSH and IKE for the same organisation, rather than treating TLS as the whole story. The question is whether posture is consistent across an estate or whether one protocol quietly lags.

The charging edge (OCPP)

Built a probe for the OCPP charging edge, where the constrained devices sit and where a migration is most likely to stop. Measurement work on whether key exchange reaches that boundary at all.

Reproducible detection evaluation

Enforced coverage floors, repeatable PCAP replay, queue-loss accounting, and prepared-environment runs. The argument is that for a detector intended for deployment, the validation method is itself the contribution.

Status, honestly: two measurement papers are in preparation, target PAM 2027. Drafts are available on request. Nothing is published yet and no results are quoted here, because a measurement paper without a completed scan is a claim without evidence — the standing blocker is research data access, not the writing.

Research interests

How I work

One worked example, including the part that does not flatter me.

Auditing my own headline

Fusion NIDS ran a six-hour soak: 87,533 flow records, zero emitted alerts, memory bounded at roughly 400 MB. The obvious way to report that is as a detection result. It is not one — the run contained no attacks, so it demonstrates pipeline stability under sustained load and nothing about detection quality.

I wrote that down and published it rather than letting the number stand unqualified. The same work records a queue-loss scenario at 99.67% loss, because a detector that silently discards traffic while reporting no alerts is indistinguishable from one that is working, and only explicit accounting tells the two apart.

The honest limitation: after that audit the project contains no detection-quality number at all. The audit disclosed an absence rather than filling it. Closing that gap is open work.
